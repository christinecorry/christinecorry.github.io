## Page-head cards (the four nav constellations)

Shown when hovering the subtitle on each page, and on the home chart when hovering the figure itself.

### About — Ursa Major, the great bear
Callisto was turned to a bear by Hera's jealousy. Years later her son, Arcas, now a hunter, met a bear in the forest that ran toward him with joy. He raised his spear. To prevent the matricide, Zeus threw them both into the stars as the Great and Little Bears. 

### Writing — Cygnus, the swan
The swan has many possible origins — Zeus in disguise to seduce Leda, queen of Sparta; perhaps Cycnus, devoted friend to the fallen Phaethon, set among the stars for his show of grief, diving again and again into the river Eridanus to gather the bones of his friend for burial.

### Curiosities — Auriga, the charioteer
Erichthonius earned his position in the sky for the invention of the quadriga (four-horse chariot). He cradles Amaltheia, the she-goat who nursed infant Zeus on Crete.

### Highlights — Cassiopeia, the queen
The queen of Aethiopia. She incurred the wrath of the Nereids and Poseidon by boasting her beauty above the sea nymphs'. Poseidon set her among the stars where she is made to wheel around the pole, half the year hanging upside down.

## Map lore (hover any other constellation on the home chart)

### Andromeda · the princess
Daughter to King Cepheus and Queen Cassiopeia. Andromeda was chained to a rock as offering to the sea monster, Cetus, then rescued by Perseus with whom later she had nine children. 

### Aquarius · the water-bearer
Because of its position in the sky through the rainy winters in Mesopotamia, Aquarius is tied to the destructive floods the Babylonians dreaded. The sky around it became "the Sea". In Greek legend, this is Ganymede, cup-bearer of the gods, said to be the most beautiful mortal alive.

### Aquila · the eagle
The eagle of Zeus, placed right next to Ganymede whom the eagle carried up to Olympus. Also the very eagle which ate Prometheus' liver each morning. 

### Antinous · the lover
A constellation which has disappeared from modern star maps, but has my favorite story! The cause of drowning for Antinous, the lover of Emperor Hadrian, is unknown; perhaps by accident, murder, or as a sacrifice to extend the emperor's life. In his grief, Hadrian founded a city on the spot, declared him a god, put his face on coins and statues across the empire, and announced a new star had appeared from Antinous's soul. Astronomers drew Antinous being carried in Aquila's talons in a recasting of Ganymede. 

### Aries · the ram
The ram of the golden fleece, later stolen by Jason and the Argonauts. The constellation is said to be dim because it gave away its golden fleece and thus shines with only the light of an ordinary sheep.

### Auriga · the charioteer  *(nav constellation — its card above is what shows; this map entry is unused)*
Erichthonius earned his position in the sky for the invention of the quadriga (four-horse chariot). He cradles Amaltheia, the she-goat who nursed infant Zeus on Crete.

### Boötes · the herdsman
Once a herdsman driving oxen around the pole, until the Wain became a bear instead of ox. Now he is one account of Arcas, son of Callisto, forever following his transformed mother through the sky. 

### Camelopardalis · the giraffe
A latecomer of 1612, drawn into the dark gap between the bears where few stars shine.

### Cancer · the crab
The crab Hera sent to nip at Heracles, crushed underfoot and pitied into the sky.

### Canes Venatici · the hunting dogs
The herdsman's dogs, forever loosed after the great bear.

### Canis Major · the great dog
The hunter's great dog; Sirius, brightest of all the fixed stars, burns at its heart.

### Canis Minor · the lesser dog
The lesser dog; Procyon rises just before the Dog Star, and is named for it.

### Capricornus · the sea-goat
Pan, half fish from diving into the Nile to escape the monster Typhon.

### Cepheus · the king
King of Aethiopia, Andromeda's father, standing watch beside his boastful queen.

### Cetus · the sea-monster
The monster sent for Andromeda, turned to stone by the Gorgon's severed head.

### Columba · the dove
The dove sent out from the ark, returning with an olive branch — or the one the Argonauts loosed to thread the Clashing Rocks.

### Coma Berenices · the queen's hair
Queen Berenice's locks, offered to the gods for her husband's safe return from war.

### Corona Borealis · the crown
Ariadne's wedding crown, flung into the sky by Dionysus.

### Corvus · the crow
The crow that dawdled and lied to Apollo, fixed thirsting beside the cup it cannot reach.

### Crater · the cup
The cup of Apollo, carried — and never delivered — by the crow.

### Delphinus · the dolphin
The dolphin that carried the singer Arion safely over the sea for the price of one last song.

### Draco · the dragon
The dragon Ladon, coiled around the pole, sleepless guardian of the golden apples.

### Equuleus · the little horse
The foal Celeris, a gift to Castor — the second-smallest figure in the sky.

### Eridanus · the river  *(not on the chart — kept for completeness)*
The river into which Phaethon fell, still burning, from the chariot of the sun.

### Fornax · the furnace
Lacaille's little chemist's furnace — a modern figure with no myth, its faint stars hiding a whole cluster of galaxies.

### Gemini · the twins
Castor and Pollux, one mortal and one divine, who refused to be parted.

### Hercules · the hero
The kneeling hero, club raised, resting between his twelve labours.

### Hydra · the water-serpent
The many-headed serpent of Lerna — the longest constellation in the sky.

### Lacerta · the lizard
A small invention of 1687, slipped between the swan and the queen.

### Leo Minor · the lesser lion
A quiet seventeenth-century filling between the lion and the great bear.

### Leo · the lion
The Nemean lion of the first labour, whose hide no weapon could pierce.

### Lepus · the hare
The hare, crouched forever at the hunter's feet.

### Libra · the scales
Once the scorpion's claws, later the balance of justice.

### Lynx · the lynx
Named, Hevelius joked, because only the lynx-eyed can trace it.

### Lyra · the lyre
The lyre Hermes strung from a tortoise shell and Orpheus played to charm stones and half-win Eurydice back from the dead. Vega was the pole star twelve thousand years ago, and will be again.

### Monoceros · the unicorn
The unicorn, pacing the winter Milky Way between the two dogs.

### Ophiuchus · the serpent-bearer
Asclepius the healer, so skilled he could raise the dead — and was made a star for it.

### Orion · the hunter
The boastful hunter, felled by the scorpion; the two are never in the sky together.

### Pegasus · the winged horse
Sprung from Medusa's blood; his great square carries the autumn sky.

### Perseus · the hero
Gorgon's head in hand — the demon-star Algol still winks within it.

### Pisces · the fishes
Aphrodite and Eros, escaped from Typhon as two fishes tied by a ribbon.

### Piscis Austrinus · the southern fish
The great fish drinking the stream poured from the water-bearer's urn. Lonely Fomalhaut is its mouth.

### Puppis · the stern
The stern of the Argo — a ship so vast that astronomers broke her into stern, sails, and keel.

### Sagitta · the arrow
The arrow — Eros' dart, or the shaft Heracles loosed at the eagle.

### Sagittarius · the archer
The centaur archer, bow drawn at the scorpion's red heart.

### Scorpius · the scorpion
The scorpion that stung Orion; red Antares is its rival heart.

### Scutum · the shield
The shield of King Sobieski, raised into the sky in 1684.

### Serpens · the serpent
The serpent twined through the healer's hands, emblem of renewal.

### Serpens · the serpent's head  *(not on the chart — kept for completeness)*
The serpent twined through the healer's hands, emblem of renewal.

### Serpens · the serpent's tail  *(not on the chart — kept for completeness)*
The serpent twined through the healer's hands, emblem of renewal.

### Sextans · the sextant
Hevelius' own instrument, set among the stars he measured with it.

### Taurus · the bull
Zeus in white-hided disguise, carrying Europa across the sea.

### Triangulum · the triangle
A simple delta — Sicily to some, the mouth of the Nile to others.

### Ursa Minor · the lesser bear
Arcas, son of Callisto.

### Virgo · the maiden
Astraea, last of the immortals to leave the earth; Spica is the wheat-ear in her hand.

### Vulpecula · the little fox
The little fox carrying a goose to the swan — Hevelius' sly invention.
